# Source Context: gokyolaketrek.com

Koray skill 1. Written 2026-08-08. **Immutable once agreed.** Every downstream
decision, the topical map, the briefs, the internal linking, takes this as input.
Changing it means rebuilding the network.

Grounded in `seo/research/operator-nepalintrepidtreks.md`, not in assumptions.

---

## Brand Identity

- **Business:** a single-trek exact-match domain selling one guided trek, the
  Gokyo Lake Trek in Nepal's Everest region, direct to the trekker. The flagship
  product is 15 days, Kathmandu to Kathmandu, high point Gokyo Ri at 5,357 m.
  Two longer variants exist (19-day with Everest Base Camp via Cho La, 21-day
  Three Passes).
- **Monetisation:** trek bookings in USD, taken direct through the enquiry form
  and WhatsApp. No ads, no affiliate. One conversion event: an enquiry that
  becomes a booking. Every page is judged on whether it moves a reader toward
  that or disqualifies them honestly.
- **Unique perspective:** an operator that actually runs this valley, not a
  content site describing it. Ten years registered (Company Registrar
  145190/072/073, incorporated 2016), Department of Tourism licence 2102, and
  first-hand Gokyo photography rather than stock. Named guides who appear in the
  reviews by name. That is the E-E-A-T asset, and it is the one thing a
  competitor aggregating trek descriptions cannot copy.
- **Primary audience:** international trekkers planning an Everest-region trek,
  in practice Taiwan, Singapore, the Philippines, the USA, Europe, the UK and
  India, judging by where the reviews come from. Fit hillwalkers with 2 to 3
  weeks, usually no prior altitude experience, choosing between this and Everest
  Base Camp.

## Central Entity

**Gokyo Lake Trek**

- **Definition:** the guided multi-day trek up the Gokyo valley in Sagarmatha
  National Park, taking in the Gokyo lakes, the Ngozumpa glacier and the Gokyo Ri
  viewpoint. Both the route and the commercial product are the same entity here,
  because the site sells the thing it describes.
- **Scope:** the route, its variants, everything a person must decide before
  walking it (season, cost, difficulty, permits, kit, acclimatisation, flights),
  everything on it (lakes, Gokyo Ri, Ngozumpa, Cho La, Renjo La, the villages),
  and the operator's own credibility.
- **Boundaries, what this entity does NOT include:** other Nepal regions
  (Annapurna, Manaslu, Langtang), peak climbing, Tibet and Bhutan tours,
  adventure activities, general Nepal tourism. Those belong to the parent
  operator's full-service site. **This is the single most important boundary in
  the project.** An EMD that drifts into being a general Nepal trekking site
  loses the topical concentration that is its only structural advantage over a
  large competitor.

### Entity disambiguation

"Gokyo" also resolves to the village, the lakes, and Gokyo Ri as separate
entities in a knowledge base. This site treats **the trek** as central and the
others as attributes of it. That matters for internal linking: a Gokyo Ri page is
a child of the trek, not a sibling.

## Central Search Intent

**Decide whether to walk the Gokyo valley, then plan and book it with
confidence.**

- **Primary predicates:** plan, compare, choose, prepare, acclimatise, cost,
  book.
- **User goal:** a reader arrives uncertain on two axes, *is this the right trek
  for me* (versus Everest Base Camp, which outranks it everywhere) and *can I
  actually do it* (altitude, fitness, Lukla flights). The site exists to resolve
  both honestly and then take the booking.

The comparison axis is the strategic core. Most searchers reaching this domain
have already heard of Everest Base Camp and are asking whether Gokyo is better.
A reviewer put the site's whole argument in one sentence: "far fewer trekkers
venture onto the Gokyo circuit."

## Site-wide N-grams

Central Entity terms combined with the intent predicates. These repeat across
every page, in boilerplate and in main content:

- Gokyo Lake Trek
- Gokyo Ri, 5,357 m
- the Gokyo lakes
- Everest region, Sagarmatha National Park
- how to plan the Gokyo Lake Trek
- what the Gokyo Lake Trek costs
- Gokyo Lake Trek itinerary, 15 days
- Gokyo versus Everest Base Camp
- Ngozumpa glacier
- acclimatisation at Namche

Every altitude, distance and duration must use one value site-wide. The
operator's own site fails this (its Gokyo meta description says "12-day" for a
15-day trek), and contradicting yourself across pages is the fastest way to lose
the entity consistency this whole model depends on.

## Quality Node Strategy

The best pages, one click from the homepage, which earn deeper crawling:

- **Node 1: the 15-day itinerary.** The product. Day by day with real altitudes,
  distances and hours. It is the page that converts and the page a competitor
  cannot fake, because it is what the operator actually runs.
- **Node 2: cost and what is included.** The highest commercial intent after the
  itinerary, and currently the biggest gap: the operator publishes no price at
  all. Whoever answers the money question properly wins the query.
- **Node 3: Gokyo versus Everest Base Camp.** The comparison the audience is
  actually running. It is also the topical border between this site and the
  larger EBC entity, so it must be argued, not hedged.
- **Node 4: Gokyo Ri.** The reason the trek exists. A destination page for the
  viewpoint, which is the strongest visual and emotional asset and the one the
  photography supports best.

## Context Shifting Rules

What this Source Context does when it meets a related topic:

**Go deep:**
- Gokyo Ri, the lakes, the Ngozumpa glacier. The central attractions, and the
  differentiators against EBC.
- Acclimatisation and altitude sickness. The trek's real risk, the reason for
  the Namche day, and a high-volume question set.
- Lukla flights, delays and cancellation. The single biggest practical anxiety
  and a real trust opportunity, because most operators are vague about who pays.
- Cost. The gap in the market and in the operator's own site.
- Cho La and Renjo La. The upsell path to the 19-day and 21-day products.

**Go shallow:**
- Kathmandu sightseeing, Nepal visa, general packing. Necessary for completeness
  and for the planning cluster, but thin, commodity, and not where authority is
  won. Cover once, well, then link.
- Everest Base Camp itself. Covered only in comparison to Gokyo, never as its own
  destination guide. Writing a full EBC guide invites the EBC entity to absorb
  this site's topical identity.

**Skip entirely:**
- Annapurna, Manaslu, Langtang and every other region.
- Peak climbing, Tibet, Bhutan, rafting, heli tours.
- General "best treks in Nepal" listicles.

These belong to the parent operator's site. Publishing them here dilutes the
Central Entity, creates cannibalisation with a site that already ranks for them,
and spends crawl budget on pages that cannot convert.

## Web components the Source Context requires

Koray's point that search engines match components to the search activity. For
this Source Context the page must let a reader *complete the real-world
behaviour*, which is planning and enquiring:

- An enquiry form with dates, group size and itinerary. **Present.**
- WhatsApp as a first-class contact, since it is how this operator actually
  converts. **Present.**
- A day-by-day itinerary table with altitude, distance and hours. **Present.**
- An altitude profile graphic. **Missing.** The single most useful component for
  an altitude-anxious buyer, and the operator's own site has one.
- A route map. **Missing.**
- A departures or availability view. **Missing**, and the operator's own site
  says "No data available", so this needs a real answer before it is built.
- A price. **Missing, and blocking.** A commercial page for a booking query
  without a price cannot fully satisfy the intent.
