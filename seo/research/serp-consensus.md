# SERP consensus and competitor mapping

Charles Floate research phase. Live SERPs pulled 2026-08-08, US location. Four
query clusters scanned, two top pages deconstructed for structure.

Everything here is from actual results. Nothing is assumed.

---

## Module 1: SERP scan

### "gokyo lake trek" (head term)

| # | Result | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | nepalhightrek.com package | operator |
| 2 | **reddit.com** guide or self-guided | forum |
| 3 | magicalnepal.com, 12 days | operator |
| 4 | backpackadventures.org, "ultimate guide" | editorial |
| 5 | **youtube.com** Gokyo Ri 360 | video |
| 6 | indiahikes.com, EBC via Gokyo Ri | operator |
| 7 | nepaltrekkingroutes.com cost blog | editorial |
| 8, 9 | **alltrails.com** | trail data |
| 10 | nepalindependentguide.com, 11 days | operator |

**Intent:** mixed commercial and informational. Google is not treating this as a
pure product query. A forum thread at 2 and a video at 5 say the searcher wants a
real opinion and a look at the place, not only a package.

**Consequence for us:** the money page has to answer the informational questions
inline, not park them on separate pages. A bare product page loses to a page that
also settles "is it worth it" and "do I need a guide".

### "gokyo lake trek cost"

Almost entirely **blog-format budget breakdowns**, not package pages:
nepaltrekkingroutes, natureexcursion, greatadventuretreks, marveladventure,
intrekking, ammoniteadventure. One operator page (adventurehimalayan).

Published price band:

| Source | Days | Price |
|---|---|---|
| nepaltrekkingroutes | n/a | $800 to $1,200 peak, $600 to $900 off-season |
| intrekking | 12 | $1,150 to $1,500 |
| marveladventure | n/a | $1,200 to $1,700 |
| natureexcursion | 12 to 14 | $1,200 to $1,800 |
| greatadventuretreks | 11 to 16 | $1,250 to $1,700 |
| adventurehimalayan | 13 | $1,375 |
| ammoniteadventure (EBC via Gokyo) | 15 to 17 | $1,600 to $2,500 |
| Reddit, real trekker | 19 | $1,300 all in |

**Consensus for a standard guided 12 to 14 day Gokyo trek: roughly USD 1,150 to
1,800.** A 15-day would sit in the upper half.

This is market context for the operator to price against. It is **not** a price
to publish. Ours must come from them.

**The strategic point:** the cost query is won by editorial breakdowns, and our
operator publishes no price at all. That is both the biggest gap and the biggest
risk. A cost page that shows a real, itemised, honest number beats six pages of
ranges.

### "gokyo lakes vs everest base camp"

| # | Result | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **reddit.com** r/everestbasecamphike | forum |
| 2 | muchbetteradventures.com | editorial |
| 3 | **facebook** group | forum |
| 4 | iantaylortrekking.com, 18 days | operator |
| 5 to 8 | frolicadventure, destinationhimalaya, acethehimalaya, himalayanwonders | operator blogs |

**Two forum results in the top three.** People do not trust operators on this
question, because the operator sells both. The winning answer has to be willing
to say who should *not* pick Gokyo.

Consensus claims across these pages: Gokyo is quieter, more scenic, arguably
harder, and the reward is a panorama rather than standing at base camp. Our own
reviewer said the same thing unprompted, which is quotable first-party evidence.

### "gokyo lake trek difficulty / altitude / permits / best time"

Blog format dominates. Universal consensus, stated identically almost everywhere:

- Best time: **spring March to May, autumn September to November**
- Difficulty: **moderately challenging**, 5 to 7 hours a day
- Gokyo Ri: **5,357 m**, no technical climbing
- Cho La: **above 5,400 m**, rocky and icy, the hard part

A Facebook post ranking at 1 for the altitude query ("first time and my
susceptibility to altitude sickness") shows the anxiety is personal and specific.
Generic AMS boilerplate will not satisfy it.

## Module 2: structural consensus

H2 patterns across ranking pages. Anything on 3+ pages is consensus and is
effectively mandatory.

**Operator package pages** (nepalhightrek is thin, only Overview / Itinerary /
Pricing / FAQs, and it ranks 1, which says the SERP is weak and beatable):

**Editorial guide** (backpackadventures, 26 headings, ranks 4 on the head term):
Why choose it, day-by-day with altitude in every heading, travel tips, permits,
guide or not, accommodation, food, when to hike, altitude sickness, books,
packing list, sustainable travel.

### Required H2 set, derived

1. Overview and why this trek
2. **Day-by-day itinerary with the altitude in each heading** (universal)
3. Cost, itemised
4. Best time and season
5. Difficulty and fitness
6. Altitude and acclimatisation
7. Permits
8. Guide or self-guided
9. Accommodation and food
10. Packing list
11. FAQs (universal)

The single strongest structural pattern: **every competitive itinerary puts the
altitude in the day heading itself**, e.g. "Day 8: Machermo to Gokyo (4750
meters)". Ours currently puts altitude in the body text. Fix that.

## Module 3: entity audit and gaps

### Entities every ranking page carries

Gokyo Ri (5,357 m), Gokyo village (4,750 m), Namche Bazaar (3,440 m), Dole,
Machhermo, Ngozumpa glacier, Cho La Pass, Sagarmatha National Park, Lukla,
Phakding, Dudh Koshi, Khumbu, Sherpa, tea house, AMS.

Our current homepage covers all of these. Baseline parity is met.

### Gaps, ranked by opportunity

1. **The lakes are not named.** Nearly every page says "six turquoise lakes" and
   stops. The individual lakes (Longpongo, Taboche Tsho, Dudh Pokhari, Thonak
   Tsho, Ngozumba Tsho) are a whole entity set sitting unclaimed on a page about
   lakes. This is the clearest entity gap in the niche.
2. **The Ramsar designation.** Only one ranking page mentions it. The Gokyo
   wetlands are a Ramsar site and among the highest freshwater lake systems in
   the world. It is a checkable, authoritative, citable fact almost nobody uses.
3. **Duration has no consensus.** Ranking pages say 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15
   days. Nobody owns "how many days does the Gokyo Lake Trek take". Available.
4. **Guide or self-guided.** Reddit ranks 2 on the head term for it. Nepal has
   required a licensed guide since 2023, so there is a correct answer, and an
   operator can give it with authority. Most operator pages dodge it because the
   answer sounds self-serving.
5. **Lukla flights, Ramechhap, cancellations.** Barely covered, and it is the
   biggest practical anxiety. Who pays for the extra night is a trust question
   nobody answers.
6. **Cost transparency.** See above.
7. **Cho La and Renjo La as the upsell.** Covered as passes, rarely framed as the
   decision "should I add this".

### Format notes

- Video ranks on the head term. An embed or an original clip is worth having.
- AllTrails ranks twice: distance, elevation gain and a map are being rewarded.
  Our route map and altitude profile are still missing, and that is a
  format-parity gap, not just a nice-to-have.
- Forums rank on the two highest-intent comparison queries. The antidote is
  visible first-hand experience and a willingness to disqualify readers.

---

## What this changes in the plan

1. The homepage day table must carry altitude in the heading, not the body.
2. Cost needs its own page and a real number, and it is the highest-value page
   on the site after the itinerary.
3. A named-lakes section, with the Ramsar fact, is a cheap and defensible
   differentiator.
4. "Do I need a guide" deserves its own page, answered straight.
5. The vs-EBC page must disqualify, not hedge, or it loses to Reddit.
6. Build the altitude profile and the route map. They are ranking factors here,
   not decoration.
